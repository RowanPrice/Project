Chemical and volume control

Boron is the slow half of reactivity control: rods handle minutes, boron handles hours. Note the moderator coefficient — heavily borated water gets less negative, and above about 1810 ppm it turns positive, at which point a rise in coolant temperature adds reactivity instead of removing it.

Heat balance

Reactor coolant pumps

Four pumps push 18 700 kg/s through the core and put about 18 MW of shaft heat into the water — which is how a cold plant is warmed up before the reactor is ever taken critical. Lose flow at power and the protection system trips at 87% of rated.

Turbine and steam dumps

Open the turbine and the secondary side cools, so the steam generators pull more heat out of the primary, so coolant temperature falls, so a negative moderator coefficient adds reactivity and power rises to meet the load on its own. The reactor follows the turbine. The dumps only pass 45% of rated flow, so a load rejection larger than that has to be matched by the rods.

Enrichment order

Separative work is real work: SWU/kg = V(xp) + (F/P−1)·V(xw) − (F/P)·V(xf), where V(x) = (2x−1)·ln(x/(1−x)). Drive the tails assay down and each kilogram of product needs less natural uranium but a great deal more separative work. Which way is cheaper depends on the two prices, and they move independently.

Core and fuel store

A reload needs 26 000 kg in store and the plant in cold shutdown below 95 °C. Cool down at more than 55 K/h and the pressure vessel accumulates thermal fatigue that no outage will undo.
